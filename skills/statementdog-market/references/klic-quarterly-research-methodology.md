# Quarterly company-to-industry methodology

Use this reference when a Statement Dog report starts from one public company's quarterly results and infers conditions for its end markets, customers, competitors, packaging/equipment chain, or adjacent suppliers. It is especially useful for packaging-equipment and Bonder quarterly reports. It records a reusable method, not current report conclusions, prices, company recommendations, or dated forecasts.

## Research model

Analyze the chain as:

`quarterly company results → product and end-market mix → customer utilization and inventory → order/backlog/qualification → peer and supplier impact → industry timing`

Do not treat one company's revenue as a complete industry indicator. First identify what it actually measures:

- current shipment or prior-period backlog;
- recurring demand or one-time revenue/expense;
- mature packaging or advanced packaging;
- memory, consumer, automotive, industrial, foundry, or OSAT exposure;
- equipment order, installation, qualification, or recognized revenue.

## Quarterly reading sequence

### 1. Normalize the reported quarter

Record fiscal versus calendar quarter, revenue, gross margin, EPS, guidance, one-time items, segment mix, and comparison basis. Strip out impairments, one-time recognition, restructuring, or unusual charges before interpreting the cycle.

### 2. Identify the demand sensor

Map each segment to end markets, customers, applications, and geographic exposure. For packaging equipment, distinguish wire bonder, TC bonder, fluxless TC bonder, hybrid bonder, test, and assembly. For each product, record whether it is mature-volume equipment or an advanced-packaging qualification opportunity.

### 3. Trace the order-to-revenue path

Use:

1. customer demand and fab/OSAT utilization;
2. customer capex plan;
3. equipment order and backlog;
4. shipment and installation;
5. process qualification or pilot production;
6. volume production and customer acceptance;
7. revenue, margin, service, and repeat order.

Backlog, announced orders, small-volume shipment, and recognized revenue are different evidence stages.

### 4. Cross-check with peers and downstream suppliers

Compare the lead company with:

- end-market semiconductor or memory makers;
- competing equipment suppliers;
- OSATs and packaging-material suppliers;
- customers named in filings or calls;
- Taiwanese downstream suppliers with the same end-market mix.

Use peers to test the mechanism, not to manufacture a recommendation. A competitor's order can confirm a market, but not the share or timing of the focal company.

## Advanced versus traditional packaging

Separate:

- mature wire-bonder demand driven by consumer, automotive, and industrial utilization;
- advanced TC bonder demand driven by HBM, 2.5D/3D, CoWoS, BSPDN, CPO, or similar packaging;
- hybrid-bonding opportunity that is still at qualification or pilot stage;
- one platform's high growth from the company's total revenue still dominated by mature equipment.

For every advanced opportunity, record pitch/IO requirement, package architecture, customer, process route, qualification stage, capacity, yield, and expected production ramp. Do not transfer advanced-packaging growth to mature equipment automatically.

## Role matrix

| Role | What to verify | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Focal equipment company | segment mix, order/backlog, utilization, guidance, one-time items, and cash flow | quarterly revenue treated as real-time end demand |
| Competing equipment supplier | customer, platform, qualification, shipment, and share | competitor order assumed to be focal-company share |
| OSAT/customer | utilization, package mix, capex, yield, and acceptance | equipment order treated as production volume |
| Memory/foundry customer | product demand, capacity, qualification, and capex | AI/HBM headline treated as immediate tool revenue |
| Material/component supplier | content, customer, qualification, utilization, and pricing | named supply-chain role assumed to be locked |

Mark every company as `explicit`, `inferred`, or `unverified`, and record the report section supporting the role.

## Forecast ledger and invalidation triggers

For every forecast, record the fiscal/calendar period, segment, end market, product, customer, qualification stage, order/backlog evidence, expected timing, one-time adjustment, company role, and status: `not_tested`, `supports`, `weakens`, `cannot_test`, or `unresolved`.

Re-evaluate the thesis when:

- one-time revenue or expense distorts the quarter;
- equipment utilization rises but orders do not follow;
- customer capex is delayed, cancelled, or redirected;
- advanced-packaging qualification fails or volume ramp slips;
- mature and advanced segments are aggregated;
- a competitor or customer shows a different end-market signal;
- supplier revenue does not follow the expected order, installation, qualification, and cash-collection sequence.

## Output requirements

For every quarterly company-to-industry report, produce normalized quarter and one-time-item check; segment/end-market/customer map; mature versus advanced product split; order/backlog/qualification/revenue evidence; peer and downstream cross-checks; margin, utilization, capex, cash flow, valuation, catalyst, invalidation, and monitoring checks. Apply the common claim types and validation statuses from the research contract. Never treat the focal company or its peer list as a recommendation.
