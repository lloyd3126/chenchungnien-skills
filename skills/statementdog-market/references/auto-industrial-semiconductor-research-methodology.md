# Automotive and industrial semiconductor methodology

Use this reference for automotive semiconductor, industrial semiconductor, analog, power, MCU, sensor, lead-frame, mature-node packaging, wire-bonder, machine-tool, and industrial-capex reports. It records a reusable method, not current report conclusions, prices, company recommendations, or dated forecasts.

## Research model

Analyze the chain as:

`vehicle/industrial end demand → distributor and customer inventory → semiconductor orders and utilization → package/lead-frame/equipment demand → supplier revenue, margin, and cash flow`

Always split:

- automotive versus industrial/automation/IoT;
- distributor inventory versus end-customer sell-through;
- inventory correction, replenishment, and real demand recovery;
- price, mix, utilization, and volume;
- semiconductor original makers versus packaging, equipment, and machine-tool suppliers.

## Inventory and end-market sequence

Track:

1. vehicle production, registrations, and OEM incentives;
2. industrial production, automation, machine-tool orders, and capital spending;
3. distributor inventory weeks and customer inventory targets;
4. semiconductor orders, cancellations, allocation, and lead times;
5. utilization, pricing, product mix, and gross margin;
6. package/test, lead-frame, wire-bonder, and machine-tool orders;
7. cash conversion, capex, and repeat demand.

Industrial demand can turn before automotive demand because its inventory correction and order cycle may start earlier. Treat this as a hypothesis to verify company by company, not as a universal lag.

## Tariff and pull-in checks

Separate:

- vehicles or industrial goods shipped before a tariff;
- distributor stock building;
- customer investment deferral;
- price increases passed to customers;
- structural electrification, automation, power, or safety content;
- true end-market recovery.

When a company reports better quarterly revenue, check inventory weeks, sell-through, backlog quality, order cancellations, geographic mix, and whether the improvement is industrial or automotive.

## Role matrix

| Role | What to verify | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Automotive semiconductor IDM | vehicle production, content/vehicle, inventory, pricing, utilization, and customer concentration | vehicle shipment growth treated as semiconductor recovery |
| Industrial/analog/power supplier | industrial orders, inventory, automation exposure, pricing, and product mix | one distributor restock treated as durable capex |
| Lead-frame/package supplier | customer, automotive/industrial mix, units, content/package, utilization, and margin | original-maker recovery assumed to flow immediately to packaging |
| Wire-bonder/equipment supplier | end-market exposure, installed base, backlog, utilization, and service | semiconductor inventory bottom treated as equipment recovery |
| Machine-tool/transmission supplier | automotive/industrial order, backlog, cancellation, utilization, and cash collection | quote activity treated as machine shipment |

Mark every company as `explicit`, `inferred`, or `unverified`, and record the report section supporting the role.

## Forecast ledger and invalidation triggers

For every forecast, record the end market, inventory stage, policy event, product mix, expected lag, customer, supplier role, timing, evidence stage, and status: `not_tested`, `supports`, `weakens`, `cannot_test`, or `unresolved`.

Re-evaluate the thesis when:

- distributor inventory weeks rise after an apparent bottom;
- customer guidance improves without sell-through or order confirmation;
- tariff pull-in is mistaken for recurring demand;
- industrial recovery does not broaden to automotive or vice versa;
- price/mix hides falling units or utilization;
- package/equipment orders lag the expected semiconductor recovery;
- capacity, customer concentration, currency, or policy changes alter margins and cash flow.

## Output requirements

For every automotive/industrial report or company, produce end-market and product mix; inventory and sell-through; price, volume, utilization, and order evidence; lead-frame, package, bonder, machine-tool, and transmission roles; margin, cash-flow, valuation, catalyst, invalidation, and monitoring checks. Apply the common claim types and validation statuses from the research contract. Never treat a concept-stock list as a recommendation.
