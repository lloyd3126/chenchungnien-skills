# Foundry research methodology

Use this reference for wafer foundry, logic manufacturing, mature-node, advanced-node, 8-inch, 12-inch, fab expansion, and foundry supply-chain reports. It records a reusable research method, not current report conclusions, prices, company recommendations, or dated forecasts.

## Research model

Analyze the chain as:

'end-market and chip demand → node and wafer-size mix → utilization and capacity allocation → wafer starts and pricing → revenue, gross margin, and depreciation → capex and fab timing → equipment, facility, and design-chain impact'

Do not analyze a foundry as one homogeneous market. At minimum split:

- advanced logic versus mature logic;
- 8-inch versus 12-inch;
- AI/HPC/server versus mobile/PC/consumer/automotive/industrial;
- volume growth versus price increase;
- existing fab utilization versus new-fab capacity;
- internal production allocation versus outsourced or transferred capacity.

## Demand and capacity sequence

### 1. Identify the demand source

Record the chip type, end market, customer, node, wafer size, and whether demand is structural or pulled forward. For AI-related demand, distinguish GPU/AI ASIC/CPU from power management, networking, analog, RF, MCU, display driver, and other mature-node peripherals.

### 2. Map the supply response

Track:

- current utilization and wafer starts;
- available cleanroom and tool capacity;
- equipment transfer between fabs or affiliated foundries;
- new fab construction, cleanroom completion, tool move-in, and production ramp;
- node qualification and customer certification;
- capacity reserved for strategic customers versus spot or non-long-term-contract business.

### 3. Test the price and mix thesis

Separate:

- price increases caused by true shortage;
- price or volume changes caused by pre-buying, tariffs, memory-cost inflation, or inventory correction;
- mix improvement from advanced nodes or HPC;
- margin change from pricing, utilization, overseas depreciation, currency, and new-fab start-up costs.

Do not infer durable pricing power from one quarter of revenue growth.

## Foundry-role matrix

| Role | What to verify | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Advanced-node foundry | node, yield, customer design wins, HPC share, capacity, and pricing | AI demand assumed to convert into immediate wafer volume |
| Mature-node foundry | 8/12-inch mix, utilization, product mix, price, and customer concentration | mature-node AI peripheral demand treated as broad recovery |
| Memory or IDMs with internal fabs | internal allocation, product priority, external foundry demand, and capex | internal capacity plan treated as available merchant-foundry supply |
| Affiliated capacity recipient | transferred equipment, qualification, ramp, and customer ownership | equipment transfer treated as immediate usable capacity |
| Equipment supplier | tool category, customer fab, order, installation, and service | foundry capex treated as recognized equipment revenue |
| Facility engineering supplier | project scope, backlog, completion, collection, and margin | fab announcement treated as recurring engineering revenue |
| IC design customer | product demand, inventory, pricing, and foundry node dependence | chip shipment growth treated as foundry order growth |

Mark each company as `explicit`, `inferred`, or `unverified`, and record the report section supporting the role.

## Timing and evidence ladder

Use this ladder:

1. end-market demand or customer forecast;
2. foundry booking, capacity reservation, or price notice;
3. utilization and wafer-start change;
4. capex approval and fab construction;
5. cleanroom and tool move-in;
6. process qualification and customer certification;
7. production ramp and product mix;
8. revenue, gross margin, cash flow, and repeat demand.

Keep pre-buying, inventory pull-forward, and true structural demand separate. When a report says a foundry may raise prices, verify the mechanism, duration, affected node, and whether customers can pass the cost through.

## Forecast ledger and invalidation triggers

For every forecast, record the claim, date, node, wafer size, end market, customer, capacity, price assumption, expected timing, evidence stage, and status: `not_tested`, `supports`, `weakens`, `cannot_test`, or `unresolved`.

Re-evaluate the thesis when:

- AI demand is concentrated in a few products or customers and does not broaden to sustainable wafer starts;
- mature-node orders are pulled forward and fall after the pre-buying window;
- utilization rises without pricing or margin improvement;
- capex or fab construction is delayed, or new tools cannot be installed and qualified;
- equipment transfer requires longer customer requalification than expected;
- new-fab depreciation, currency, tariffs, or start-up costs offset volume growth;
- customer inventory, memory costs, tariffs, or consumer price increases weaken mobile/PC demand;
- a foundry loses node yield, customer design wins, or capacity allocation to a competing foundry;
- equipment, facility, or IC-design suppliers do not show the expected follow-through.

## Output requirements

For every foundry report or company, produce:

1. end-market, customer, node, and wafer-size scope;
2. utilization, capacity, pricing, and mix thesis;
3. capex, fab, cleanroom, tool, qualification, and ramp timing;
4. advanced versus mature and AI versus consumer exposure;
5. equipment, facility, and design-chain roles;
6. revenue, margin, depreciation, cash flow, and valuation checks;
7. catalysts, invalidation triggers, and time horizon;
8. historical report claims separated from current metrics and official disclosures.

Apply the common claim types and validation statuses from the research contract. Never treat a foundry concept-stock list as a recommendation.
