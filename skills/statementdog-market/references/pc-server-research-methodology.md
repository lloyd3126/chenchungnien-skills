# PC and server research methodology

Use this reference for PC, traditional server, AI server, server OEM, server ODM, CPU/GPU, enterprise SSD, and memory reports. It records a reusable demand and platform-transition method, not current report conclusions, prices, company recommendations, or dated forecasts.

## Research model

Analyze the chain as:

`end-market and customer capex → platform/order timing → system shipment and rack integration → CPU/GPU/memory/storage content → supplier revenue, margin, inventory, and cash flow`

Always split:

- PC versus traditional server versus AI server;
- unit shipment versus system value and content per system;
- customer order, backlog, shipment, installation, and revenue recognition;
- real sell-through versus tariff/policy pull-in;
- compute, networking, memory, storage, power, and cooling bottlenecks.

## Demand and platform checks

### 1. PC

Track consumer and commercial units, channel inventory, replacement cycle, operating-system transition, tariff exposure, average selling price, and customer confidence. A pull-in can raise one quarter's shipments while reducing the following quarter's demand.

### 2. Traditional server

Track enterprise and cloud demand, CPU platform cycle, refresh timing, storage/network content, geographic mix, backlog, and customer capex. Traditional server demand may be weaker even while AI server demand is strong.

### 3. AI server

Track accelerator availability, rack configuration, system integration, networking, HBM/DRAM, enterprise SSD, power/cooling, customer qualification, and deployment timing. A strong GPU order does not automatically mean recognized server or component revenue if integration yield, delivery, or customer acceptance is delayed.

## Pull-in, backlog, and shipment sequence

For every forecast, map:

1. customer capex or end-market order;
2. system order and backlog;
3. component allocation and inventory;
4. platform integration, rack assembly, and yield;
5. shipment and installation;
6. customer acceptance and revenue recognition;
7. follow-on orders, utilization, margin, and cash conversion.

Separate backlog from demand. Ask whether backlog is cancellable, whether it is for a specific platform, and whether the customer has accepted the system.

## Role matrix

| Role | What to verify | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| PC brand | units, channel inventory, ASP, commercial/consumer mix, and guidance | pull-in treated as replacement-cycle recovery |
| Server brand/ODM | AI/traditional mix, backlog, rack integration, customer, geography, and margin | GPU order treated as server revenue |
| CPU/GPU supplier | platform shipment, allocation, inventory, customer qualification, and product transition | announced platform treated as end-customer deployment |
| Memory/SSD supplier | content per system, product mix, qualification, ASP, inventory, and capacity | server value growth assumed to equal memory bits or SSD units |
| Power/cooling/network supplier | rack content, qualification, bottleneck, utilization, and recurring demand | one rack design assumed to remain standard |
| Component or assembly supplier | customer, platform, content, yield, capacity, and cash collection | system shipment assumed to flow equally to all suppliers |

Mark every company as `explicit`, `inferred`, or `unverified`, and record the report section supporting the role.

## Forecast ledger and invalidation triggers

For every forecast, record the market, platform, customer, unit/content assumption, pull-in risk, qualification stage, timing, company role, evidence stage, and status: `not_tested`, `supports`, `weakens`, `cannot_test`, or `unresolved`.

Re-evaluate the thesis when:

- PC or server pull-in is followed by channel/customer inventory build;
- AI server backlog does not convert to shipment, installation, or acceptance;
- accelerator, HBM, networking, power, cooling, or rack integration bottlenecks delay deployment;
- traditional server and AI server are incorrectly aggregated;
- customer capex is concentrated or reduced;
- platform transition changes content, ASP, gross margin, or supplier share;
- component revenue rises without utilization, cash flow, or repeat-order support.

## Output requirements

For every PC/server report or company, produce market/platform mix; unit, content, and ASP assumptions; customer capex, backlog, inventory, integration, and qualification evidence; CPU/GPU/memory/storage/power/network roles; margin, cash-flow, valuation, and monitoring checks; catalysts, invalidation triggers, and time horizon. Apply the common claim types and validation statuses from the research contract. Never treat a platform or concept-stock list as a recommendation.
