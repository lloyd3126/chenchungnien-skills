# Workflow productization methodology

Use this reference when a company claims that data, software, hardware, services, or professional expertise form an embedded workflow with recurring revenue and switching costs.

## Research model

Separate the stack:

`input/data → analysis or planning → decision/workflow → execution/output → feedback data`

Then test the commercial loop:

`entry product → installed use → downstream attach/renewal → customer outcome → retention and expansion`

Recurring billing alone does not prove productization. A workflow product should become repeatable without proportional custom labour, improve a measurable customer outcome, and retain customers for reasons stronger than contractual friction.

## Sequence

### 1. Map the workflow and decision owner

Identify each step, user, economic buyer, data input, handoff, failure cost, and alternative process. Mark which layer the company owns and where customers can export data or replace a component.

### 2. Separate the product layers

Distinguish data, research/insight, decision software, workflow integration, hardware entry point, consumable/output, and human service. Assign revenue, gross margin, labour, capital, and renewal behavior to each layer rather than calling the bundle “software.”

### 3. Measure productization

Track recurring or subscription mix, gross retention, net retention where meaningful, implementation time, support labour per customer, revenue per employee, deployment reuse, update cost, and gross margin. Compare organic growth with acquired growth and project revenue.

### 4. Prove customer outcome and attach

Measure time saved, throughput, error/default reduction, revenue uplift, compliance outcome, or asset utilization. For an entry product, calculate installed-base utilization and downstream attach; ownership of hardware without downstream usage is not workflow control.

### 5. Test switching cost and interoperability

Separate data history, integration, training, regulation, network acceptance, clinical/operational risk, and commercial bundling. Check data portability, third-party interfaces, multi-homing, and whether a competitor can replace only the highest-value layer.

## Role-specific checks

| Workflow | Primary checks | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Data/risk decision | recurring mix, renewal, data coverage, decision adoption, regulatory use | data subscription called indispensable workflow |
| Hardware-to-consumable | installed base, utilization, attach, output volume, interoperability | hardware shipments treated as downstream lock-in |
| Industrial ecosystem | connected assets, active usage, paid acres/assets, service outcome | connected equipment mistaken for monetized usage |
| Vertical software | retention, support intensity, customer outcome, organic growth | acquired recurring revenue mistaken for product growth |

## Invalidation triggers

- support or implementation labour scales with revenue;
- recurring revenue is maintained by acquisition rather than organic renewal and expansion;
- installed hardware does not increase downstream utilization or output revenue;
- data volume rises without better customer outcomes or willingness to pay;
- third-party interoperability removes the entry product's attach advantage;
- customers retain the product only because migration is temporarily costly, while new wins deteriorate.

## Output requirements

Produce a workflow-layer map, revenue/cost by layer, productization scorecard, customer-outcome evidence, attach or renewal bridge, switching-cost decomposition, and the smallest observation that would disprove workflow control.
