# Capital-efficiency and ROIC research methodology

Use this reference when evaluating whether a business converts its operating advantage into returns on the capital required to sustain and grow it. Do not rely on a single company-wide ROIC when segments have different risk, capital intensity, or accounting treatment.

## Research model

Start with:

`ROIC = normalized after-tax operating profit ÷ operating invested capital`

Then separate:

- maintenance capital from growth capital;
- minimum operating working capital from excess cash;
- operating assets from goodwill and acquisition accounting;
- agency capital from lending, inventory, guarantee, or platform capital;
- current ROIC from incremental or reinvestment ROIC.

## Sequence

### 1. Segment the business

Group activities by economic model, not only by financial-statement label. A light-asset agency, a lending business, an inventory business, and an acquired software portfolio require different denominators and risk adjustments.

### 2. Normalize operating profit

Remove clearly non-recurring items, separate SBC and acquisition costs according to the research question, adjust for unusual cycles, and apply a defensible effective tax rate. Explain every adjustment and preserve the reported number beside the normalized number.

### 3. Build invested capital and two acquisition views

Use operating fixed assets, required working capital, inventory, receivables, loan balances, guarantees, and other assets that support the segment. Do not automatically count excess cash. Where the balance sheet overstates or understates required operating capital, estimate a minimum operating level and show the range.

For acquired businesses, show both views instead of selecting the more flattering denominator:

- **Organic operating ROIC** excludes acquired goodwill and purchase-accounting intangibles to test the operating asset engine.
- **Acquisition-inclusive ROIC** includes purchase price, goodwill, identifiable intangibles, and acquisition debt where relevant to test the allocator's actual return.

Reconcile why the views differ and use acquisition-inclusive returns when evaluating management's capital-allocation record.

### 4. Compare returns with reinvestment

Calculate current ROIC, average-cycle ROIC, incremental ROIC, maintenance cash return, and the amount that can be reinvested at attractive returns. A high current ROIC with no reinvestment runway may be a harvesting business, not a compounder.

## Role-specific metrics

| Business | Primary checks | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Asset-light agency/platform | take rate, SG&A/GMV, working capital, minimum capital | revenue margin mistaken for return on capital |
| Hardware/industrial | utilization, fixed assets, inventory, capex, service mix | peak-cycle margin used as normalized return |
| Finance | loan balance, yield, funding cost, loss, capital, leverage | spread income before losses and capital |
| Serial acquirer | FCF, maintenance capex, purchase price, goodwill, debt | reported EPS growth from leverage or accounting |
| Hotel/loyalty | fee income, contract capital, points liabilities, working capital | franchise model treated as zero-capital |

## Invalidation triggers

- ROIC falls when normalized working capital or maintenance capex is included;
- incremental ROIC is below the cost of capital;
- current returns depend on peak pricing, temporary utilization, or under-reserved losses;
- acquisition goodwill and debt rise faster than cash earnings;
- capital intensity shifts because guarantees, inventory, lending, or owned assets expand;
- cash return is lower than accounting return for multiple cycles.

## Output requirements

Show reported and normalized profit, the invested-capital formula, segment ROIC, incremental ROIC, maintenance versus growth investment, cycle range, and the reinvestment runway. For acquired businesses, include organic operating and acquisition-inclusive ROIC. State which inputs are directly disclosed, article-derived, calculated, or estimated.
