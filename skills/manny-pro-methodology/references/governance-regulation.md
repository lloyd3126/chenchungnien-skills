# Governance and regulation research methodology

Use this reference when the business depends on licenses, standards, institutional trust, moderation, professional judgment, public policy, or a regulated transaction. Treat regulation as part of the business model rather than a footnote to the risk section.

## Research model

Trace:

`institutional rule → permitted activity → compliance cost → participant trust → market access → economic rent`

Separate four questions:

- Does regulation create a moat or only a barrier that any licensed competitor can obtain?
- Does the rule protect quality, limit supply, or redistribute value between participants?
- What is the cost of compliance, remediation, monitoring, and political uncertainty?
- Does a change in law alter the addressable market, unit economics, or capital requirements?

## Sequence

### 1. Build the regulatory timeline

Record licenses, standards, legal classifications, enforcement events, historical crises, policy changes, and the company's responses. Keep historical context separate from current law. In current-state mode, verify current rules against dated official sources. In source-bound mode, report only the article/archive-era state and label current status unverified.

### 2. Identify the protected bottleneck

Determine whether the moat comes from accreditation, data, professional expertise, reputation, distribution, switching costs, or simply a limited license. Test whether a new entrant, incumbent, or regulator can reproduce or remove it.

### 3. Model compliance and conduct

Measure compliance headcount, technology, reserves, moderation, audit, insurance, legal spend, customer verification, and remediation. For platforms, map Admin/Moderator/User authority and incentives. For regulated firms, map supervision and reporting obligations.

### 4. Design state-based scenarios

Use states such as licensed, restricted, delayed, challenged, or prohibited. For each state specify probability only when evidence supports it; otherwise show a range and the operational trigger that moves the company between states.

For market-entry analysis, keep at least these gates separate: `legally permitted → implementing rules effective → license awarded → product operational → customer adoption → profitable after tax/compliance`. A legal market or theoretical TAM is not operating revenue.

## Role-specific metrics

| Business | Primary checks | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Rating/data intermediary | accreditation, methodology, default history, legal exposure, switching cost | regulation mistaken for permanent pricing power |
| Online gambling | licensed states, tax/fee burden, market access, responsible-gaming cost, enforcement | legal addressable market treated as immediate revenue |
| Social platform | moderation cost, policy changes, content quality, privacy/data restrictions | engagement growth despite trust deterioration |
| Auction/alternative asset | provenance, AML/KYC, import/export, tax, freeport rules | brand trust assumed to remove compliance risk |

## Invalidation triggers

- a legal change removes exclusivity or expands low-cost competition;
- compliance cost rises faster than revenue or user value;
- a license is delayed, restricted, revoked, or made conditional;
- enforcement, litigation, fraud, or moderation failures damage trust;
- the business model depends on a contested legal interpretation;
- political or social pressure changes customer behavior before formal law changes.

## Output requirements

Provide a timeline, legal-state date, market-entry gate, protected mechanism, compliance cost, scenario table, affected revenue/capital items, and a specific monitoring trigger. Do not give legal advice. Cite the relevant official rule only when directly checked; otherwise label the statement article-derived, archive-era, or unresolved.
