# Growth-quality research methodology

Use this reference when a company reports attractive growth but the source of that growth is unclear. Separate price, volume, mix, market expansion, share gains, penetration, acquisitions, and accounting effects before judging quality.

## Research model

Build the bridge:

`revenue growth = price + volume + mix + new customers/locations + market share + acquisitions + FX/accounting effects`

Then connect it to:

`growth → incremental gross profit → incremental operating profit → incremental cash flow → reinvestment required`

Growth is valuable only when the incremental return exceeds the cost of capital and the economics can persist.

## Sequence

### 1. Define the starting base

Use the same segment, geography, currency, accounting scope, and period. Exclude acquired revenue, discontinued operations, one-time contracts, and currency effects before comparing organic growth.

### 2. Decompose price, volume, and mix

For each segment, identify units, ASP, utilization, customer count, frequency, product mix, channel mix, and geography. Explain whether a higher ASP reflects pricing power, inflation pass-through, premium mix, shortage, or a temporary comparison effect.

### 3. Separate share from market growth

Estimate the relevant market and compare company growth with market growth. State when market size is an external estimate, when share is inferred, and when the company serves a different product or geography than the headline market.

### 4. Test cash and reinvestment quality

Compare incremental operating profit with incremental working capital, capex, acquisitions, stock compensation, and customer incentives. A growth story that requires more capital every year may be a lower-quality compounder than the margin profile suggests.

### 5. Pair leading and lagging indicators

For each reported outcome, identify the operating signal expected to change first. Examples include billings/deferred revenue before recognized revenue, dealer inventory and 30+ day delinquency before equipment sales, installed-base utilization before downstream consumables, or traffic/retention before ARPU. Preserve management-defined KPI formulas over time; if the definition changes, bridge the old and new series rather than treating them as continuous.

## Role-specific metrics

| Growth source | Primary checks | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Price | price realization, contract repricing, elasticity, churn | shortage or inflation mistaken for durable pricing power |
| Volume | units, transactions, active users, rooms, handle, cases | inventory pull-forward or low-quality volume |
| Mix | product, customer, geography, channel, premium tier | mix benefit assumed to be permanent |
| Penetration | installed base, attach rate, adoption, repeat | total addressable market used without conversion evidence |
| Share | comparable market, competitor growth, win/loss | market definition changes with the conclusion |
| Acquisition | acquired revenue, retention, purchase price, synergy, dilution | acquired growth counted as organic compounding |

## Invalidation triggers

- revenue growth falls after price normalization while units remain weak;
- margins rise but cash conversion, receivables, inventory, or deferred revenue deteriorate;
- growth depends on one customer, one product, one geography, or one regulatory regime;
- share gains are not visible in competitor disclosures, customer wins, or capacity;
- incremental ROIC falls below the cost of capital;
- management repeatedly changes the definition of organic growth or addressable market.

## Output requirements

Present a growth bridge, the evidence for each driver, incremental margin/cash conversion, reinvestment needs, persistence horizon, and paired leading/lagging metrics that distinguish durable growth from pull-forward or mix effects. Disclose KPI definition changes and unresolved reconciliation gaps.
