# Alternative-asset research methodology

Use this reference for art, collectibles, rare goods, unique real assets, or other markets where scarcity, identity, narrative, and transaction design matter more than a standard production-cost model. It is also useful when a company intermediates, finances, authenticates, or stores such assets.

## Research model

Separate four values:

1. use or consumption value;
2. social, cultural, or identity value;
3. expected financial resale value;
4. option or collateral value.

Then trace:

`scarcity + information asymmetry + trust + buyer competition → price discovery → realized price − transaction friction`

An observed auction price is not automatically intrinsic value, liquidity, or a repeatable return.

## Sequence

### 1. Define the asset and market stage

Separate primary creation from secondary resale, blue-chip from speculative segments, public auction from private sale, and high-liquidity from locked-up assets. Record whether the item is unique, substitutable, authenticated, divisible, or reproducible.

### 2. Analyze price discovery

Track estimate, reserve, hammer price, buyer's premium, seller's commission, unsold rate, bidder count, repeat sale, provenance, venue, and publicity. Test anchoring, FOMO, winner's curse, selection bias, and auction-house effects.

### 3. Calculate net investment economics

Subtract commission, tax, insurance, storage, transport, financing, authentication, maintenance, and time out of market. Use repeat-sales data or comparable indices carefully; the sample can be biased toward assets that actually trade.

### 4. Test liquidity and institutional constraints

Measure sell-through, time to sell, bid/ask spread where available, supply lock-up, tax incentives, inheritance, museum acquisition, freeports, AML/KYC, and collateral lending. Market value can rise while realizable value falls.

## Role-specific metrics

| Asset/business | Primary checks | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Art/collectibles | repeat-sale return, volatility, supply, sell-through, fees, provenance | index return without selection and friction adjustment |
| Auction house | GMV, commission rate, guarantees, private sales, inventory, financing | record lot mistaken for recurring growth |
| Asset-backed finance | LTV, collateral haircut, spread, default, recovery time | appraisal treated as liquidation value |
| Rare/unique goods | scarcity, authenticity, buyer depth, replacement, storage | narrative premium treated as fundamental value |

## Invalidation triggers

- supply increases or authenticity is challenged;
- buyer concentration or narrative sponsorship weakens;
- realized prices fall after fees, taxes, and holding cost;
- repeat-sale evidence disappears outside selected top lots;
- regulation or tax rules reduce holding or trading incentives;
- lenders, auction houses, or museums change collateral or acquisition standards.

## Output requirements

Separate use, identity, financial, and collateral value; provide gross and net return; state liquidity and selection limitations; identify price-discovery mechanisms and the event that would break the narrative.
