# Embedded-finance research methodology

Use this reference when a company attaches lending, payments, insurance, credit, or financing to a core product, marketplace, dealer network, or customer relationship. Treat the financial product as a separate business with its own capital, risk, and cycle.

## Research model

Analyze two connected engines:

`core product adoption → financing penetration → loan balance/transactions → yield and fees → credit losses/funding cost → customer lifetime value`

The financing product may increase conversion and retention, but it can also turn a light-asset operating model into a balance-sheet and regulatory business.

## Sequence

### 1. Map the embedded use case

Identify who borrows, why the financing is convenient, whether it is necessary to complete the core transaction, what collateral or recourse exists, and whether the customer can obtain an equivalent product elsewhere.

### 2. Measure penetration and balances

Separate stock from flow:

- loan portfolio or receivable balance;
- gross originations and repayments;
- financing penetration = gross originations ÷ core GMV or eligible transactions;
- average balance and turnover;
- customer and product concentration.

### 3. Reconstruct risk-adjusted economics

Use:

`asset yield − funding cost − expected credit loss − servicing/compliance cost = risk-adjusted spread`

Add capital requirements, collateral haircuts, liquidity, securitization, guarantees, and recovery value. Do not treat interest revenue as operating profit before loss and capital.

### 4. Test the double engine

Ask whether financing improves the core business or merely grows alongside it. Compare conversion, average ticket, repeat use, delinquency, loss, and core customer economics between financed and unfinanced cohorts.

## Role-specific metrics

| Embedded product | Primary metrics | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Dealer/equipment finance | penetration, wholesale receivables/equipment sales, dealer inventory, NIM, 30+ days past due, provisions, residual value | loan growth during a credit bubble |
| Marketplace/art finance | loan-to-value, originations/GMV, spread, collateral liquidity, bad debt | high-value collateral assumed liquid in a downturn |
| Buy-now-pay-later | approval, conversion, repeat, funding cost, loss, provisions | GMV uplift before lifetime loss is known |
| Payment/credit product | active accounts, spend, take rate, fraud, credit loss, capital | transaction growth hiding credit risk |

## Invalidation triggers

- penetration rises only because underwriting standards weaken;
- receivables or loan balances grow faster than core transactions;
- delinquency, provisions, charge-offs, or collateral haircuts rise after a lag;
- funding cost or securitization access worsens;
- financed customers have worse retention or contribution after losses;
- regulators impose capital, disclosure, fee, or licensing requirements.

## Output requirements

Show the core-to-finance funnel, stock/flow definitions, risk-adjusted spread, penetration ceiling, capital requirement, credit-cycle stress case, and whether the financing business deserves a separate valuation or ROIC calculation. For dealer finance, use wholesale receivables relative to equipment sales, dealer inventory, 30+ days past due, and provision speed as leading indicators rather than waiting for reported equipment revenue to decline.
