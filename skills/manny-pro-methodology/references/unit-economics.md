# Unit-economics research methodology

Use this reference when aggregate financials hide the economics of a store, user, case, stake, loan, machine, room, property, shipment, or transaction. The unit must reflect how management can actually add capacity or make a decision.

## Research model

Choose the smallest repeatable economic unit and trace:

`unit volume × unit price → unit revenue → variable cost → contribution → fixed-cost absorption → maintenance capital → cash return`

Do not use a convenient unit that is not operationally controllable. A company can have excellent aggregate margins while newly added units destroy value.

## Sequence

### 1. Define the unit and cohort

Specify geography, product, customer type, age, channel, and whether the unit is new or mature. Keep mature-unit economics separate from expansion-unit economics.

### 2. Build contribution economics

Calculate revenue, directly variable costs, labour or service cost, marketing/acquisition cost, payment/commission cost, and contribution profit. Allocate fixed costs only when the allocation reflects an actual capacity decision.

### 3. Add time and capital

Measure opening investment, ramp time, maintenance capex, working capital, replacement cycle, payback period, and cash yield. A high accounting margin does not prove a high cash return if the unit consumes inventory, equipment, or customer acquisition capital.

### 4. Compare cohorts and saturation

Compare new versus mature units, domestic versus overseas, high-value versus low-value users, and organic versus acquired cohorts. Look for density, cannibalization, learning curves, churn, and fixed-cost operating leverage.

## Role-specific metrics

| Unit | Primary metrics | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant/store | same-store sales, traffic, basket, gross margin, labour/rent, payback, closure rate | total store growth hiding weak new stores |
| Medical/software case | cases, utilization, ASP, doctor/customer adoption, repeat, support cost | shipment growth without completed or paid cases |
| Social user | DAU/MAU, engagement, ARPU, CAC, retention, content cost | user growth purchased with low-value traffic |
| Betting customer/stake | handle, hold, net gaming revenue, LTV, UAC, payback, bonus cost | gross handle mistaken for profit |
| Loan/finance unit | average balance, yield, funding cost, loss rate, capital, recovery | spread income before credit losses and capital |
| Hotel room/property | occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, fee/contract, owner economics, maintenance capex | owner unit economics mixed with brand/manager fee economics |

## Invalidation triggers

- new-unit payback lengthens while mature units appear stable;
- volume grows but price, mix, retention, or contribution falls;
- variable costs are actually semi-fixed and scale does not improve margins;
- customer acquisition or incentives rise faster than lifetime contribution;
- maintenance capex or working capital is omitted from cash return;
- cohort quality deteriorates after expansion to a new geography or channel.

## Output requirements

State the unit definition, cohort boundary, formula, assumptions, payback, capital requirement, mature/new comparison, and the operational trigger that would stop expansion. When the company does not disclose a required input, use a range and label the proxy. For franchise, management, or asset-light systems, calculate the owner/operator unit and the brand/platform unit separately; expansion is not durable if only the platform earns an acceptable return.
