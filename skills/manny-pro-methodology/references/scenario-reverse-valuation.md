# Scenario and reverse-valuation research methodology

Use this reference when the user wants to know what a current valuation already assumes, whether a business can grow into its price, or which observable events would invalidate the thesis. It is a decision framework, not a promise of a target price.

## Research model

Run two directions:

1. **Forward**: operating drivers → revenue → margin → cash flow → capital → value.
2. **Reverse**: price/value → implied revenue, margin, ROIC, reinvestment, terminal growth, and duration.

The key question is not whether a company is “good,” but whether the current price requires assumptions that are plausible, already priced in, or vulnerable to a specific counterexample.

## Sequence

### 1. Define the valuation object

Record share price, market capitalization, enterprise value, net cash/debt, diluted shares, valuation date, currency, and whether the multiple is based on reported, normalized, or forward earnings. Keep the date fixed.

If a dated price or equivalent valuation input is absent from the allowed sources, stop short of a current valuation conclusion. Provide the driver model, equation, and required-input table so the analysis can be rerun when the user supplies a price. Never silently substitute an article-era price for a current one.

### 2. Build a driver tree

Use the correct operating model: GMV × take rate, users × ARPU, price × volume × mix, store/unit cohorts, loan spread, fee income, or acquisition FCF. Connect each driver to margin, working capital, capex, dilution, and capital cost.

### 3. Reverse the valuation

Solve for one or more implied assumptions:

- required revenue or FCF CAGR;
- sustainable operating margin or take rate;
- duration of above-market growth;
- reinvestment rate and incremental ROIC;
- terminal growth and terminal margin;
- acceptable credit loss, dilution, or acquisition pace.

When an exact solution is not possible, show a range and identify the dominant assumption.

### 4. Build scenarios and falsifiers

At minimum use base, upside, and downside cases. Vary the assumptions that have economic meaning, not arbitrary percentage points. For each case specify what must be true, what would confirm it, what would disconfirm it, and when the evidence should arrive.

### 5. Monitor the thesis

Create a small dashboard of 3–7 leading indicators. A good indicator changes before reported earnings and maps directly to the driver tree: volume, utilization, penetration, price, mix, customer retention, inventory, credit loss, capital spending, or acquisition yield.

## Invalidation triggers

- the implied growth rate is higher than the addressable market or historical execution without a new mechanism;
- growth requires declining take rate, rising incentives, or increasing capital intensity;
- reported earnings meet estimates while cash conversion, working capital, or dilution worsens;
- a key regulatory, product, customer, or acquisition assumption changes;
- the market price rises but the required future assumptions become less plausible;
- the thesis cannot specify an observable disconfirming event.

## Output requirements

Present valuation date and input provenance, forward driver tree, reverse-implied assumptions, three scenarios, sensitivity to the dominant variables, falsifiers, and monitoring cadence. Do not report a precise target price without showing the assumptions that generate it; when dated inputs are missing, label the output a valuation framework rather than a current valuation.
